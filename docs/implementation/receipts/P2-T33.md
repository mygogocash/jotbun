- **What changed:**
  - The rules (`lockModel.ts`, pure, P4c). `lockReducer` as W18 writes it: with the lock on, `start` locks; `background` covers and locks, with no grace time; `inactive` only covers, so the Face ID prompt cannot lock again; `active` uncovers and leaves a lock in place; `unlocked` unlocks and keeps a cover; `disabled` clears both. With the lock off nothing locks or covers. `appStateEvent` makes active, inactive and background events and says nothing for any other status. `afterAnswer` and `afterRecheck` are R67's answers (below). `namedMethod` gives the phone's method, or the passcode while the phone has not said. `METHOD_KEYS` gives each method's name, line and body keys. `lockSwitch` gives the setup switch's checked, disabled and needs-passcode state.
  - The port (`localAuthPort.ts`, P1). `LocalAuthPort` has `availability()`, read every time it is asked, and `authenticate(words)`. `noLocalAuth` cannot lock and answers every prompt `unavailable`. `getAppLocalAuth()` returns `noLocalAuth` until P2-T34. `fakeLocalAuth(results, availability)` serves the tests and the catalogue.
  - The gate (`LockGate.tsx`, P2, P4b, P5, P6). It reads the stored setting (`jotbun.wallet.lock`) first. Until the setting is read, and when it cannot be read, the app is covered and nothing asks, so a broken store never opens the app. It follows AppState through `lockReducer`. Each opening with the lock on (a `start`, every `background`) asks the phone once, when the app is in front. The prompt's own `inactive` asks nothing, a press while a prompt is up asks nothing (one in-flight guard), and an answer to an earlier opening is dropped (an opening counter). R67's answers: `success` unlocks; `cancelled` and `failed` stay locked with `lock.tryAgain`; `lockout` stays locked with `lock.lockout`; `unavailable` reads the phone again. A phone still able to lock stays locked with `lock.tryAgain`. A phone no longer able to lock has its stored setting removed, is unlocked, and shows `lock.turnedOff` once as a notice at the top (toast colours, polite, a 44 pt close). A prompt that throws counts as `failed`, and a phone that cannot be read counts as still able. Covered or locked, the app stays mounted under a wrapper that screen readers do not reach, and `LockCoverContext` says `hidden`, so every Tray and Sheet draws the cover. On Android, `BackHandler` returns true while hidden. The gate says each note on iOS once, with `announcePolitely`. It provides `LockSettingContext`, read through `useLockSetting()`: the setup screen and the row read `ready`, `enabled`, `availability` and `busy`, and call `change(on)` (asks the phone first, both ways; turning on needs a phone that has said it can lock), `answer()` (keeps `lock-answered`) and `recheck()`. Outside a gate the lock reads off on a phone that cannot lock.
  - The cover (`lockCover.tsx`, P2, P11). `LockCover` gains an optional `screen`. `LockCoverArt` draws the ground (`aria-hidden`, test id `lock-cover`) and, over it, the lock screen while the app is locked. The topmost cover is therefore the one a person sees and presses (P2-T13's review, m1). `Tray.tsx` and its tests are unchanged.
  - The locked screen (`LockedScreen.tsx`, P7; sa-locked). The padlock Bun is a decorative sticker. `lock.lockedTitle` is the h1 heading. The method's line sits in `quietOnGround`, and the note, when there is one, sits in a polite status region. At the foot, `PrimaryButton` `lock.unlock` names the method, and, except on a passcode phone, `lock.usePasscode` is an ink `TextLink`. Both open the same prompt. The screen scrolls and draws only the ground behind it.
  - Setup (`LockSetupView.tsx`, P4a, P8; sa-lock-setup; `LockSetupScreen.tsx`, P13). The view has the back `IconButton` (`common.back`). With `setup`, `lock.step` shows in violet. The question is the heading, and the method's body follows it. The switch row is `role="switch"`, named `lock.switch`, hinted with the fallback line, with a 52 by 32 pt track. `lock.needsPasscode` shows when the phone cannot lock.
