- Where: `src/v1/routes.ts:135` gives `'attachments.prepare': {}`, so `UserVault#call` runs `assertSession` and, for this `current_policy` route, `assertPolicyAccepted` before `prepareReceipt` (`src/vault/user-vault.ts:146-148`). - My mutant X1 sets `'attachments.prepare': { anySession: true }`. All 52 committed cases still pass. Three of my probes fail on it, and all three pass on `a3493f4`:
  - AC-1, a signed-out session;
  - AC-2, a session without the current terms (terms v2 published);
  - AC-3, an account in `deleting`.

  - The brief's P6 pins the session checks of release (M47) and of the download step (M48), but not prepare's. Retain and replace are covered by `runCommand`'s own checks, so the same mutant there is equivalent.

## What holds

### 1. Security and access

- **Owner, live session, policy.**
  - Every route reaches only `idFromName(claims.accountId)`. Another account's record or image is the same 404 as an unknown one (the two ported ownership cases).
  - Retain and replace carry `access: 'current_policy'` into every step (`vaultSession`). Prepare and the two command steps run `assertSession` and `assertPolicyAccepted` in `call()`, and `runCommand` runs both again because the kinds are `policy: 'current'`.
  - Remove is one `current_policy` vault call. Route access, gates and status are pinned against the Nest decorators by `test/vault-route-coverage.test.ts` and `test/route-coverage.test.ts` (Node).
  - Probes AC-1 and AC-2 pass on `a3493f4`: 401 or 403, and nothing reserved or copied (but see m-1).
- **A deleting account and an erased vault refuse.**
  - Probe AC-3 marks the account `deleting`. Retain, replace, remove, the grant and the download all answer 403 `ACCOUNT_UNAVAILABLE`, and the registry is unchanged. The `attachments.release` step still runs (anySession) and leaves a published receipt alone.
  - Probe AC-4 erases the vault. Retain, the grant and a grant issued earlier all answer 403.
- **Grants.**
  - A grant is issued under `vaultGrants(c, 'attachment')` with `receiptGrantScope(c.claims)` (account and session) for `{ snapshotId: attachmentId, index: 0 }`. It lasts `ATTACHMENT_GRANT_MINUTES` on the vault clock: five minutes and one second later it is 404.
  - A second session of the same person is 404 (M1 killed). Another account is 404, the same body as for an unknown id. A deleted record's receipt is 404 (M23).
  - `receiptFile` re-runs `held()` at download time, so a grant serves only the receipt it names, while it is retained and a live record holds it.
- **Downloads.**
  - The object key comes from the `attachments` row, published in the same block that recorded it. `matchingObject` refuses a size or digest mismatch with 404, and the stream is digest-checked with the last chunk held back (M42).
  - `RECEIPT_SLOTS.take(w.claims.accountId)` is taken after the vault step (M39, M40; my X12, which takes it before the step, is killed by eight cases).
  - No amount, key, grant or body is logged. The new code has no logging, and failures reach `toProblemReply` and `errorResponse`, which log only the error's name.

### 2. The copy path

- **P3.**
  - `COPY_SLOTS.take()` comes before each `attachments.prepare` (M30, M31 killed). `none`, `wait` and a 429 give the slot back in `finally`.
  - A busy isolate answers 503 with 5 seconds before anything is reserved.
  - Refusals prepare can foresee (resend, existing receipt, stale version, wrong holder state, deleted or unknown record, unsupported schema, consent or id) reserve nothing, so they spend no daily budget. The refused-retains case counts zero receipt writes, and the budget case counts rows by `created_at`.
  - A tombstone outlives its reservation's 24-hour window, so released copies keep counting.
- **P5.** `copyOf` passes one `AbortSignal.timeout(copyDeadlineMs)` to both `copy` and `head`, and races the pair against it in `beforeAbort` (M32 killed). My X21 removes the race and is killed by the one-deadline case.
- **P1.**
  - The publish runs in the command step, a later vault call whose clock is read when its block starts, so it is always after the PUT and the HEAD.
  - `ready()` refuses with 422 `errors.attachment_expired` before `publishObject` in five cases:
    - the reservation is not still `reserved` with `delete_after` ahead;
    - `created_at + 180 s` is not ahead (even when a failed R2 delete moved the deadline on);
    - the key is outside `receipt/<vault>/<attachment>/`;
    - the extension differs;
    - the HEAD's size or digest is not the import image's registered pair.
  - The kit kills M2 and M14 to M17; I reran M2, M14 and M15, and they were killed again.
- **No orphan past its deadline.**
  - A failed copy, a source that vanished and a refused command each give the reservation back at once (M33, M34, M35). A copy made for an existing receipt is released inside the command (M19).
  - A Worker that dies leaves its reservation due at three minutes. A PUT that lands after the key was given up is caught by B19's tombstone a day later.
  - Until 1B-T11 wires `deleteDueObjects` and `attachmentsDueAt` into the alarm, due copies are not deleted. That is the plan's order, and the routes are not live before 1B-T13.

### 3. R-B (P2)

- **Structure.** `prepareReceipt` is synchronous SQL inside `call()`'s one `transactionSync`, with no `await`. The command's publish, the `attachments` insert and the holder row are in one block too, so no request can see "copy published, receipt not yet recorded".
- **Probes, all passing on `a3493f4`:**
  - RB-1: ten concurrent `attachments.prepare` steps for one image answer `copy` once and `wait` nine times, with one registry row.
  - RB-2: the first request is held after its HEAD, before its command. The second is told `wait` repeatedly (observed through a `VAULT` wrapper) and then `none`. Both answer 200, with one receipt write and one published row.
  - RB-3: the first is held after its HEAD while the vault clock passes its reservation (+3 min 5 s). The second reserves and publishes its own copy. The first's command then finds the receipt and attaches it (200). The result is one `attachments` row and one published key, with the first copy reserved and due.
  - RB-4: the first record is edited while its copy is in flight. The first command answers 409 and releases its copy, and the waiting second reserves its own copy and wins. The result is two writes, one published, one due.
- **Mutants.** M5 is killed by the deterministic one-block case. M38 is killed by the ported case, but see n-4.

## Notes for later tasks

- **fix-download-aborts (R97):**
  - Apply the `waitUntil` wrapper to `serveReceiptFile` as written. `RECEIPT_SLOTS` allows two per account, so two interrupted receipt downloads close that member's receipts in the isolate.
  - Retain and replace hold a `COPY_SLOTS` slot across several awaits (`receipts.ts:104-111`). If workerd drops a request's pending work when its client disconnects mid-copy, as it does a download's body cancel, that slot would never come back, and four such requests would close copying in the isolate.
  - I could not test this in the Workers runtime. Add it to the task's `wrangler dev` proof: four retains whose client aborts mid-copy, then a retain answers 200. If it leaks, the same wrapper fixes it.
- **1B-T10:**

- **1B-T11:**

  - `touch(c)` after `attachments.prepare` and `attachments.release` does not arm the alarm yet. Once the alarm runs `deleteDueObjects`, an abandoned or released copy should bring it within its deadline.
