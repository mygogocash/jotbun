### Major

**MAJ-1. The masked-number regex is quadratic. A 1 MB CSV holds the parser child until its 30 s deadline.**

- Where: `apps/api/src/imports/parsers/bank.ts:54`
  `const MASKED_NUMBER = /(?<![A-Za-z0-9])[xX*](?:[\s-]*[xX*])+((?:[\s-]*[0-9]){4,})/;`
  It runs on every preamble cell (`bank.ts:64`). `table.ts:198` reaches it for any bank header without a wallet column.
- Cause: the lookbehind only rules out a start after a letter or digit, so a match can start at every `*` in a run of `*`, and after every `-` or space in an `x-x-x...` or `x x x...` run. At each of those starts, `(?:[\s-]*[xX*])+` eats the rest of the run and then backtracks one step at a time. The work grows with the square of the cell length.
- Evidence, `probes/redos.mjs` on `head/` (one cell):
  - 10,000 `*`: 368 ms.
  - 5,000 `x-`: 88 ms.
  - 5,000 `x `: 86 ms.
  - 10,000 `x`: 0.1 ms (the lookbehind holds for runs of `x`).
- Evidence, `probes/runner-probe.cjs`: the built, sandboxed table child from `head/apps/api/dist`, with one preamble row of N cells of 10,000 `*`, then the Thai bank header and one data row.

  | File | Size | Time | Result |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `stars20` | 200 KB | 7.4 s CPU | result |
  | `stars82` | 820 KB | 29.9 s CPU | result (once, earlier, `timeout`) |
  | `stars100` | 1 MB | 30.1 s | `{"kind":"timeout"}`, which becomes `parse_timeout` |
  | Ordinary 4,994-row statement | 10.8 MB | 162 ms CPU | result |

  The same kinds of probes on the base parser (long amount cells, digit and comma runs) stay linear (milliseconds).
- Impact:
  - A signed-in user can turn a 1 MB upload into a full parser deadline. That is about 200 times the cost of the largest ordinary file.
  - The parse server runs one parse at a time (`parse-server.ts` header: other requests get `retry` `not_started`), and the PostgreSQL path runs one parser for the whole account base (`apps/edge/src/roles/parser.ts`, `PARSER_NAME`). So this delays every other account's imports.
  - `IMPORT_QUOTAS.parseJobsPerDay` (10) lets one account hold the parser about 5 minutes a day. Several accounts multiply that.
  - Memory stays in budget (59 MB peak), and nothing throws.
  - The reviewer brief named "very long cells" as a hostile-input check. This is new code, and the fix is a few lines in the task's own file.
- Fix:
  - Bound the scan in `preambleAccountLabel`. Either test only cells up to a small length (an account cell is short, for example 64 characters), or replace the regex with a linear scan that does not restart inside a mask run.
  - Add a `bank-statements.spec.ts` case: a preamble of long `*`, `x-` and `x ` runs (for example 100 cells of 10,000 characters) gives a result inside the deadline with a null label. Assert a usage or time bound, such as `usage.cpu_ms` under 2,000.
  - Keep the existing helper cases passing. Add a mutant: drop the bound, and the new case fails.

**MIN-2. A bank export has no day-first fallback (plan silent; record it, do not change it without a ruling).**

- `table.ts:191` gives `detectDateOrder` the `dmy` fallback only for the legacy layouts (`DAY_FIRST`). A bank export's layout is `generic`.
- So in a statement whose days are all 12 or less (for example one downloaded on 12 October for 1 to 12 October), every date is `ambiguous_date`. Probe `smallDays`: three rows `01/10/69`, `05/10/69`, `12/10/69`, all `ambiguous_date`.
- Thai bank exports are day-first. This can bite the owner's proof (Q10).
- The plan does not say, so it is not a defect against the plan. Add it to the receipt's notes as a risk, or raise it with the owner (a `dmy` fallback for bank headers would be a one-line change in `buildRows`).

### Thai bank CSV handling

Probes ran through the real sandboxed child (`probes/runner-probe.cjs`, plus earlier probes with the same content):

- **Preamble and masked account.**
  - `xxx-x-x1234-x` gives `บัญชีออมทรัพย์ xxx-1234`.
  - Upper-case `XXX-X-X1234-X` gives `Savings Account xxx-1234`.
  - Card-style `**** **** 1234 5678` gives `xxx-5678`.
  - `x1234`, `xx-123`, `Fax 0212345678` and `Taxx 1234` give null.
  - An unmasked number never matches. Only the last four digits ever leave the preamble.
- **Header search.**
  - A header on row 30 (index 29) is found. One on row 31 gives `unrecognized_columns`.
  - Lone `วันที่` and `ยอดเงิน` preamble rows are passed over.
  - Record numbers are kept (`row:8` first in `bankCsvTh`).
- **Money columns.**
  - `"-1,500.00"` withdrawn is expense `150000` (sign ignored). `"25,000.00"` deposited is income `2500000`.
  - `0.00`, `-0.00`, `-`, ` 0 `, `฿0.00` and `THB 0.00` hold no amount.
  - Both columns filled gives `type_conflict`; neither gives `missing_amount`.
  - `(12.50)` is expense `1250`. `+5` is expense `500`.
  - `=1+1` and `@x` hold text: the type is kept and the row is flagged `invalid_amount`.
  - Formula-looking descriptors and details (`=HYPERLINK(x)`, `@SUM(A1)`, `-cmd`, `+shop`) pass through as text. They are no amount.
- **Dates and era (today 2026-10-25).**
  - `24/10/69` gives 2026-10-24; `24/10/26` gives 2026-10-24; a file with both gives `ambiguous_date` on every row.
  - `24 ต.ค. 69` and `Oct 24, 26` read in the era.
  - `29/02/67` gives 2024-02-29 (a BE leap year, checked in CE). `29/02/68` gives `invalid_date`. `32/10/69` gives `invalid_date`.
  - `26/10/69` (tomorrow) gives `date_out_of_range`.
- **Across the year boundary (today 2026-01-05).** `30/12/68`, `31/12/68`, `01/01/69` and `04/01/69` give 2025-12-30, 2025-12-31, 2026-01-01 and 2026-01-04.
