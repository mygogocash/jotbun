### Nit

**n1. A crash report sent without consent carries the flag values PostHog puts on every event.**
- **Evidence:**
  - PostHog adds `$feature/<key>` and `$active_feature_flags` to every event from its stored flags (`posthog-core.ts:213-227`).
  - `updateFlags` persists them (`:930-936`), and the client keeps the default `'file'` persistence (`posthog-rn.d.ts:12-14`). So they come back at the next launch, and stay until the first `setAccount` resets the client, which waits on `/v1/me` (m3).
  - `scrubException` keeps every property but `$exception*` and `$issue*` (`gate.ts:71-74`).
  - Crash reports leave without consent unless `EXPO_PUBLIC_CRASH_REPORTS_WITHOUT_CONSENT` is `'0'` (`env.ts:15`).
  - Probe 1's last case documents it: a no-consent `$exception` keeps both properties.
- **When it happens:** only while the client still holds the last yes's flags outside yes. That is a launch before the reset, the whole of an offline launch, and `loading`.
  - 1D already accepts the persisted distinct id on such reports in the same window, so this adds little.
  - It is the class of leak P4 cares about.
- **Fix (optional):** when `!policy.analytics`, `scrubException` drops `$active_feature_flags` and every `$feature/` key. Then rollout membership never rides on a report sent without consent, whatever the client holds.
