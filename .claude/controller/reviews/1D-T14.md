## The controller's focus, answered

- **What reaches analytics.**
  - The emitters take only `method`, and the allow-list keeps only `method` from its enumeration (`catalog.ts:63`, `:121-123`).
  - PN1 records every cleaned event through `setConsentedTelemetrySink`, held or sent. Email, Google and Apple sign-ups and sign-outs each carry exactly `method`.
  - The JSON of everything tracked and sent contains none of these: the address, the Apple subject and relay address, the Google subject and address, any token, session id, account id or stored email.
  - An unanswered account's held events are dropped at its sign-out (PN5). Only the account that said yes is sent.
  - The emitters cannot throw into the provider: nothing in `catalog`, `bus` or `events` throws, and `bus` wraps the controller. So analytics never blocks a sign-in or a sign-out.
- **The sign-in event (P2, P3).**
  - `session.created` decides (`credentialProvider.ts:249`; K23 through `ServerSession.created`). `signUp: true` never does: M2 is killed by 9 cases.
  - It is tracked synchronously after the `bootstrapped` dispatch. The dispatch notifies the seam, and `accountFollower` makes its `setAccount(null, { signedOut: true })` call before its first await (`AnalyticsUpkeep.tsx:49-53`); `setAccount` with null never awaits (`controller.ts:211-231`). So by the next line the controller holds no one, and the event is held for the new account's yes.
  - No event is ever captured under the previous account. The planner's switch case (stand-in) and PR1 (real `AnalyticsUpkeep`) both give `reset, reset, identify, signed_up`. In PN3 (a sign-out while a switch runs), A's `signed_out` goes once, under A, and the beaten B sends nothing. A joined sign-in shares one flow, so it sends one event.
- **The sign-out event (P4, followups line 770).**
  - It goes first, before `endSession()` dispatches (`:468-469`), and only from `active`.
  - Locked (a token read, or a refused refresh), `reauth_required` (`SESSION_REQUIRED`, `ACCOUNT_UNAVAILABLE`), a sign-out during a first sign-in, a second tap, and an unanswered account: none of them sends or holds anything the next account's yes would send. The evidence: the planner's case 5; PN2, PN4, PN5 and PN6; PR2 on the real `AnalyticsUpkeep`.
  - The active guard is the planner's, beyond the plan's literal rule (P4). It is the plan's own D37 rule, and M5 shows the literal rule leaks the ended account's `signed_out` into the next account's yes. I agree with it.
- **R125 (P10).**
  - Sign out everywhere passes `{ event: false }` through `settleEverywhere` (`SettingsScreen.tsx:235`, `:250-251`), and Try again's resend uses the same `settleEverywhere` (`:278-281`). PR3, with a lost answer and then `accepted`, or then `SIGNED_OUT`, sends no `signed_out`. Privacy's Done also passes it (`PrivacyScreen.tsx:438`).
  - Settings' plain Sign out still sends: the planner's render case 1, with M10 and R7 killed.
  - The option reaches the provider through `signOutOfThisDevice` (`SettingsParts.tsx:63-66`; M12 killed). Both services wire that function itself (`accountServices.ts:27`, `privacyServices.ts:61`), and the app has no other caller of the provider's `signOut`.
  - Where the option is lost: m1.
  - The brief's own edge (P10): after a sign out everywhere whose diary erase fails, the plain Sign out that the note asks for sends `signed_out` (PR4 proves it). This follows R125's last sentence, and the receipt notes it, so I raise nothing.
- **The stand-in follower (P5).** It mirrors `accountFollower` as P5 says. The render file adds the sign-out order and R125's screen paths on the real component, but not M5 or M6: see m2.
